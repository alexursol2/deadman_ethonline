# ETHGlobal Project Check-in #2

**Project:** Deadman — an x402 escrow whose refund is executed by the network, not by a keeper.
**Due:** Friday 11 September, 05:59. **Track:** Hedera, AI & Agentic Payments. Second partner: Privy.

---

## Text to paste

> **Live and working.** The service is deployed and public:
> **https://deadman-server.onrender.com** (x402 endpoint) and
> **https://deadman-board.onrender.com** (live board). A real x402 payment settles through the
> Blocky402 facilitator into our escrow contract (`0.0.10426758`), the escrow arms its own refund
> with the Hedera network in the next transaction, and if the seller never reveals the decryption
> key the network executes that refund on its own — `signatures: []`, no keeper, no cron, nobody
> pushing a button. Verified end to end against the public deployment, not just locally. The board
> reads the mirror node directly from the browser and keeps working while our own server is down,
> which is the point of the demo.
>
> **What we finished since check-in #1.** The escrow contract with mutation-tested adversarial
> coverage (53 tests, each proven to fail against a deliberately broken build before it counts); the
> x402 resource server and a paying agent; `verify.ts`, a buyer-side tool that catches a lying seller
> and names which of four commitments was broken, demonstrated against three real cheats where the
> chain is happy and the seller is paid. Our own adversarial suite found and fixed a real
> vulnerability in our contract along the way, which is written up rather than quietly patched.
>
> **Privy is integrated and there is a finding for them.** The paying agent signs through a Privy
> server wallet — no private key exists on our machines — and a full purchase ran on it against the
> public deployment. Getting there needed a bridge worth reporting: x402 on Hedera pays with a
> native `TransferTransaction`, not an EVM transaction, so Privy's EVM signing methods are the wrong
> shape; the raw `secp256k1Sign` primitive fits Hedera's `keccak256(body)` + `r||s` scheme exactly.
> Two things we could not work around. Privy policies are **default-deny**, so a policy written as
> DENY rules bricks the wallet including the transfers it was meant to permit. And **a spend cap
> cannot bind this rail at all**: no `PolicyMethod` covers raw signing, so the only rule that permits
> the signature x402 needs is `ALLOW *`, which also lets an over-cap transfer through — we measured
> 10 HBAR going out under a 6 HBAR cap. We shipped a wildcard whose rule name states the limitation
> rather than a cap that looks like protection and provides none. A policy method for raw signing,
> or conditions over Hedera transaction bodies, would close this.
>
> **Blocker 1 — the Hedera questions from check-in #1 are still unanswered.** We now have six, and
> two of them are gaps in Hedera's own stack rather than ours: (a) x402 on Hedera **cannot settle
> into a contract call** — `@x402/hedera` only builds a `TransferTransaction` and its facilitator
> rejects anything else by name — so no escrow, vault or streaming product on that rail can take
> payment atomically, and we can point at the exact line; (b) network-executed calls do **not**
> appear under the mirror node's `/contracts/{address}/results`, so a scheduled call that fired and
> reverted is invisible where any dashboard would look; (c) a rejected `deleteSchedule` returns
> transaction SUCCESS with the refusal only in the return code, and Hedera's own
> `payments-scheduler` template ships a helper that discards it; (d) `scheduleCall` reverts with
> empty returndata when starved of gas, contradicting how HIP-1215's "never reverts" reads, with a
> ~1.45M gas floor; (e) the delete authorisation model is undocumented — we established it
> empirically; (f) `hasScheduleCapacity(now + 1)` returns false despite the HIP stating a
> one-second minimum. We would like these in front of a Hedera engineer before Sunday.
>
> **Blocker 2 — Blocky402 remains a single-vendor hard gate**, unchanged from check-in #1. It is up
> and has behaved correctly in every test, but it is one hosted service with no public source and no
> status page, and settling through it is a track requirement. If it goes down during judging we
> cannot substitute it and would fail qualification for reasons outside our control. Still asking
> whether there is a contact or a fallback position.
>
> **Not blocked on:** anything of our own. Public deployment is live, the repo has real commit
> history, and we are on schedule to submit Saturday night.
